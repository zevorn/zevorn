# English Writing Guide

## Evidence status

The current corpus provides strong evidence for Zevorn's Chinese voice but limited evidence for a distinct English prose style. Do not translate Chinese cadence sentence by sentence or claim that a provisional English draft perfectly reproduces Zevorn's English voice.

Until Zevorn supplies manually edited English articles:

- preserve the cross-language traits supported by the corpus;
- use idiomatic English structure for the selected genre;
- ask for a target publication, audience, or representative English sample only when it would materially change the result;
- learn from Zevorn's later English revisions and promote only deliberate, repeated choices.

English cohesion does not relax the truth boundary. When the user supplies a closed fact list:

- do not turn a passing test into an unprovided claim about what the system proved;
- do not invent why the narrator held an initial hypothesis;
- do not add a generic mechanism as though it were part of the narrator's reasoning;
- do not broaden a supplied takeaway into a claim about what an Agent or a team generally does.
- do not add intent through modifiers such as `deliberately`, `intentionally`, `naturally`, or `surprisingly` unless the source provides it;
- do not infer the psychological purpose or effect of a person's words, even when the inference sounds considerate.

If explanatory context is necessary, label it as general context and keep it separate from Zevorn's first-person record. Prefer omission when the requested article is already understandable.

Do not use general engineering claims to fill a requested word count. A shorter source-faithful article is better than a complete-looking article padded with plausible context.

## Cross-language traits to preserve

- Start from an observed event, concrete problem, person, object, or result.
- Move through time and causality rather than presenting a finished taxonomy.
- Keep first-person uncertainty, revised judgment, and incomplete understanding when they are true.
- Put evidence near technical claims and details before emotional conclusions.
- Use restrained language and state the supported scope.
- Create warmth through attention to people and ordinary actions, not sentimental adjectives.
- End at the scale of the actual experience or result.

## Tone and degree

Use `I think`, `probably`, `may`, `might`, `so far`, `a little`, `not quite`, and similar qualifiers only when they accurately mark uncertainty or degree.

Avoid unnecessary absolutes such as `always`, `never`, `completely`, `the only`, `undeniably`, and unsupported superlatives. Do not hedge a verified fact, measured result, reproducible instruction, or necessary safety requirement.

Prefer calm, considerate statements over rhetorical certainty. When disagreeing, describe the competing intuition fairly and name the condition under which it may still hold.

## Sentences and paragraphs

Do not reproduce Chinese line breaks mechanically.

- Use cohesive paragraphs with a clear local subject.
- Keep a mechanism, example, and its explanation together.
- Use a shorter sentence for a genuine turn, admission, risk, or landing.
- Vary sentence length, but avoid a stream of one-sentence paragraphs.
- Repeat the noun when an English pronoun would make the referent unclear.
- Use topic sentences when they help a technical or argumentative reader, but do not make every paragraph sound like an essay template.

## Transitions

Choose transitions by their English function rather than as direct translations:

- `later` or a dated event for chronology;
- `eventually` after real duration or repeated attempts;
- `fortunately` only when a concrete risk has passed;
- `but` for a direct turn;
- `however` for a more formal qualification;
- `in fact` for correction supported by evidence;
- `of course` only for a genuine shared premise or brief aside;
- `still` or `yet` for a restrained unresolved contrast;
- `in the end` only for an actual outcome, not a manufactured lesson.

Avoid repeated paragraph openings with transitions. English often carries logic through subject order, verb choice, and paragraph structure without an explicit marker.

## Genre calibration

### Personal essays

Use scene, time, weather, movement, or an object to enter. Keep paragraphs somewhat more cohesive than the Chinese WeChat cadence. Let humor remain brief and adapt culture-specific memes rather than translating them literally.

### Technical writing

Use explicit subjects, evidence, conditions, versions, and limitations. Keep commands, logs, and code next to the question they answer. Prefer medium paragraphs that explain one mechanism.

### Opinion and retrospectives

Develop the judgment through a specific case, a competing intuition, and a boundary. End with the present choice, remaining uncertainty, or next action.

## English review checklist

- [ ] Is the draft idiomatic English rather than translated Chinese syntax?
- [ ] Are claims limited to the supplied evidence?
- [ ] Did any causal phrase, intention adverb, or interpretation add a fact that was not supplied?
- [ ] Did I turn the user's specific takeaway into a broader claim about Agents, teams, or engineers?
- [ ] Are qualifiers accurate rather than habitual?
- [ ] Does each paragraph have a clear local subject and purpose?
- [ ] Are transitions functional rather than repetitive?
- [ ] Does warmth come from attention and detail?
- [ ] Does the ending stop before it restates the lesson?
- [ ] If personal English voice matters, is there enough Zevorn-edited English evidence to support it?
